# Background Literature Review on an Elderly-Friendly Food and Medication Logging Application

## 1. Introduction

Elderly patients often have multiple health conditions and chronic diseases, which may require them to manage several medications, follow specific dietary requirements, and monitor health-related habits such as diet. However, these daily health routines can be difficult to maintain consistently. Seniors may face challenges such as forgetfulness, which can affect medication adherence and increase the risk of missed doses, over-dosing, or under-dosing. They may also have difficulty recalling what they have eaten or understanding whether their meals meet their dietary needs, making it harder to maintain a balanced diet.

A food and medication logging app can help address these challenges by supporting seniors in recording their food intake, receiving medication reminders, and keeping track of what has been taken and when. Such apps can also support chronic disease management by helping users monitor daily habits that may affect long-term conditions such as hypertension or diabetes. However, such an app must be designed around seniors’ daily routines, abilities, and limitations rather than simply adapted from general health tracking applications. This is especially important because elderly users may face barriers such as low digital literacy, difficulty learning new app functions, and accessibility issues when using digital health tools.

This background report reviews the key considerations involved in designing a food and medication logging application for elderly users. It first examines the challenges seniors may face when using food and medication logging apps, such as difficulty remembering to log, the burden of manual input, and low digital confidence. The report then considers how accessible design principles can make such applications easier for seniors to use. In addition, it reviews existing food tracking and medication management applications, identifies useful features that can improve medication adherence and engagement in diet tracking, and discusses gaps in current solutions.

## 2. Elderly User Challenges in Food and Medication Logging Apps

For food logging, one major challenge is that elderly may find diet self-monitoring burdensome. Aure et al. (2021) reported that some participants felt constrained by the need to carry their device with them to record meals, while others were unable to log their diet consistently because they forgot or lacked the energy to do so. This suggests that food logging apps for seniors should reduce the amount of manual logging and input from users. Instead of expecting elderly users to record every meal, snack, or beverage in detail, the app could allow diet tracking to be narrowed to selected meals, such as dinner. Steinert et al. (2016) found that using an app to record simple health-promoting activities, such as eating fish twice per week, can support behavioural change through limited and personally tailored goals. This suggests that diet quality could be evaluated using simple food group coverage, such as whether the user has consumed vegetables, fruits, protein, or grains, rather than relying on detailed calorie counting, which may be unnecessary for older adults. Additionally, De Santis et al. (2022) highlight that digital technologies for older adults should make their potential health benefits explicit. Therefore, the app should explain the purpose of food logging clearly, such as showing users how balanced meals and missing food groups may relate to their overall health.

Yap et al. (2016) identify memory-related issues and complex dosing regimens as key reasons for medication non-adherence among elderly patients. This is especially relevant for seniors who manage multiple medications at different times of the day, as they may forget whether they have taken a dose or become confused by complicated medication schedules. As a result, a medication logging app should reduce memory burden by providing clear reminders, simple confirmation of completed doses, and an easy-to-view record of what medication was taken and when.

## 3. Design Considerations for Elderly-Friendly Apps

An app designed for elderly users should prioritise visual clarity and simplicity. Farage et al. (2012) emphasise that interfaces for older adults should use high contrast, readable text, simple visual presentation, and avoid unnecessary visual clutter. Where appropriate, warm colours can also be used, as older adults may prefer colours at the long-wavelength end of the spectrum. The interface should use font sizes of 12 points or larger, clear text, large buttons, and sufficient spacing between interactive elements so that seniors can read information comfortably and tap buttons accurately. Error prevention should also be considered, such as using confirmation prompts before recording medication intake to reduce accidental taps, duplicate entries, or incorrect logs.

The app should also be designed with simple and intuitive procedures. Farage et al. (2012) suggest that tasks for older adults should be broken down into simple, discrete and minimal steps, while navigation buttons and commands should remain consistent throughout the interface. Commonly used actions should be readily visible and accessible and the app should avoid deeply nested functions or complicated menus, as these may make it harder for seniors to remember where features are located. Manual typing should also be minimised by allowing users to choose from predefined options as text entry may be challenging for elderly users.

## 4. Existing Food and Medication Logging Applications

To better understand the current landscape, it is useful to review existing applications that support food and medication tracking. Examining their features and limitations provides insights into what is already available and highlights areas where improvements can be made. Based on their primary functions, these applications can be broadly grouped into food logging applications, medication management applications, and applications that support both food and medication tracking.

## 4.1 Food Logging Applications

Food logging applications such as MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, and Lose It! are primarily designed to help users monitor their dietary intake (Zečević et al., 2021). Many applications support manual food entry and barcode scanning, while some also provide photo-based logging to simplify meal logging (Dugas et al., 2026).

In terms of functionality, MyFitnessPal and Lose It! mainly emphasise calorie counting and weight management, while Cronometer focuses on detailed nutrient analysis (MyFitnessPal, n.d.; Lose It!, n.d.; Cronometer, n.d.). While these features provide comprehensive dietary monitoring, their emphasis on detailed nutritional analysis may be less relevant for elderly whose primary goal is to monitor their daily eating habits rather than achieve weight management goals (Gomez-Hernandez et al., 2023).

## 4.2 Medication Logging Applications

Medication logging applications such as Medisafe, MyTherapy, and CareClinic are developed to support users in managing their medications and maintaining adherence to prescribed treatment plans (Lanke et al., 2025). Common features include medication reminders, personalised dosing schedules, manual medication entry, and the ability to log whether scheduled doses have been taken or missed (Dayer et al., 2013).

Besides these core functions, these applications place emphasis on different aspects of care. Medisafe includes caregiver support, MyTherapy combines medication management with health tracking, and CareClinic integrates symptom and lifestyle monitoring alongside medication records (Medisafe, n.d.; MyTherapy, n.d.; CareClinic, n.d.). This reflects a broader shift towards comprehensive medication management, extending beyond reminders to support users with complex treatment needs.

## 4.3 Integrated Food and Medication Management Applications

Unlike standalone food or medication logging applications, applications such as MyNetDiary and Health2Sync combine both functions within a single platform, allowing users to manage multiple aspects of their health without switching between separate applications (MyNetDiary, n.d.; Health2Sync, n.d.).

In addition to these integrated functions, both applications include features tailored to chronic disease management (MyNetDiary, n.d.; Health2Sync, n.d.). For example, they integrate meal records with blood glucose monitoring to help users with diabetes understand how dietary choices and medication use affect their condition over time (Chang et al., 2022). By presenting these records together, users can more easily identify patterns and monitor the impact of their daily habits on disease management (Health2Sync, n.d.). However, their emphasis on chronic disease management may limit their suitability for older adults seeking a simple, general-purpose food and medication logging application.

## 5. Research Gap and Opportunity

Existing applications tend to focus on either diet tracking or medication management. While integrated platforms are available, they are often designed for specific conditions or broader health management.

Many existing food logging applications emphasise calorie counting and weight management and often require extensive manual input. Gioia et al. (2021) suggest that often users may need to manually record meals and recall details such as what and when they ate, which can be challenging for elderly individuals with memory-related difficulties. Furthermore, detailed nutritional information may be unnecessary for older adults whose primary goal is to maintain a balanced diet.

Tabi et al. (2019) suggest that most medication management applications primarily focus on reminder systems and adherence tracking. While these functions are important, they are often implemented independently from other health-related behaviours. As a result, elderly users may need to switch between multiple applications to manage different aspects of their health, increasing complexity and reducing convenience.

Therefore, there is a need to develop a food and medication logging application specifically designed for elderly users. Such an application should prioritise simplicity, accessibility, and ease of use while supporting the key health management tasks that older adults perform daily.

## 6. Proposed Features for Improving Adherence and Engagement

The proposed application is designed based on the challenges identified in Section 2 and the research gap in Section 5. It aims to reduce manual effort, improve usability, and integrate food and medication tracking into a single elderly-friendly platform. The features are divided into primary and secondary features.

## 6.1 Primary Features

### Food Tracker

The food tracking feature consists of **Photo Logging** and **Food Group Tagging** which allows users to record meals by taking photos instead of manually typing food entries. This addresses the issue of high manual input effort identified in Section 2, where elderly users may struggle with recalling and entering detailed food information. After uploading a meal image, users can tag food groups such as fruits, vegetables, proteins, and carbohydrates. This simplifies dietary tracking by removing the need for calorie counting or complex nutritional analysis, which is often unnecessary for older adults whose main goal is balanced nutrition. In addition, this supports better dietary awareness in a more accessible and intuitive manner compared to traditional food logging applications.

### Medication Tracker

The medication tracking feature enables users to input medication details such as name, dosage, and frequency, supporting both **Reminders** and **Completion logging**. The system then generates reminders based on scheduled times, helping to address forgetfulness and complex medication routines identified among elderly users in Section 2. When medication is taken, users can mark it as completed, and the system records the date and time automatically. This reduces confusion about whether a dose has already been taken and supports accurate medication adherence tracking.

### Accessibility Design

The application incorporates accessibility-focused design principles such as large text, high-contrast colours, clear layouts, and simple navigation structures. Liu et al. (2021) suggest that this directly addresses age-related challenges such as reduced vision and lower digital literacy. By minimising nested functions and ensuring key features are easily accessible, the app reduces learning difficulty and improves usability for elderly users.

## 6.2 Secondary Features

### Customisable Dietary Tags

This feature allows users to define or adjust food group tags based on their dietary needs or cultural preferences. This provides flexibility while still maintaining simplicity in food logging. It ensures that the system can adapt to individual dietary requirements without increasing complexity in the core interface.

### AI-Assisted Food Tagging

To further reduce manual effort, the application suggests possible food group tags based on uploaded meal images. However, users are still required to manually confirm the final selection. This balances automation with user control, ensuring ease of use while maintaining accuracy in food tracking. It reduces cognitive effort, which is beneficial for elderly users who may find manual categorisation challenging.

### Medication Verification via Photo Capture

When marking medication as completed, users are required to take a photo of the medication. This provides an additional layer of confirmation to reduce accidental or incorrect logging. It supports better accuracy in medication adherence tracking and helps address forgetfulness or uncertainty about whether medication has been taken.

### Habit Streaks and Progress Tracking

The application includes simple habit tracking features such as consecutive days of medication adherence and consistent food logging. Habidu. (2026) suggest that this encourages routine building and long-term engagement. Additionally, tracking balanced meal days, based on food group coverage, helps users visually understand their dietary consistency in a simple way.

### Caregiver Notification System

If medication is not taken after reminders, the system can notify caregivers. This feature addresses the risk of missed medication doses in elderly users with higher levels of dependency. It also supports external monitoring, which can improve safety and adherence for users who require additional assistance.
